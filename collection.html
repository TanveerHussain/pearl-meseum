How a pearl is formed

Built layer by layer, in the dark.

Inside its shell, a pearl oyster lines its home with nacre: the lustrous mother-of-pearl you see here.

When something unwanted lodges inside it, the oyster may coat it in the same nacre, one fine layer over another, for years. A natural pearl is that patience, made solid.

Mother-of-pearl, the nacre lining a pearl oyster’s shell.

Understanding pearls

What makes a pearl special?

Five qualities, read together. No single one decides a pearl’s worth.

  1. i.Size and weight
  2. ii.Shape
  3. iii.Colour
  4. iv.Lustre
  5. v.Surface quality

i. Size and weight

Every pearl was weighed.

Size and weight come first. In general, the larger a natural pearl, the rarer it is.

Merchants weighed pearls on small, finely balanced scales, against tiny brass weights.

ii. Shape

Few are perfectly round.

A perfectly round natural pearl is rare. Most grow into a shape of their own.

iii. Colour

Each pearl wears two colours.

Its body colour, and a faint overtone that seems to float over it, such as rose or silver. Gulf pearls are often white to cream, many with a soft rose overtone.

  • White

    White, with a cool silver overtone

  • Cream

    Cream, warm and soft

  • Rose

    Rose, a white body with a pink overtone

  • Silver

    Silver, pale grey with a green sheen

iv. Lustre

Watch the light move.

Lustre is light, returned from deep inside the nacre.

The deeper the layers, the deeper the glow.

v. Surface quality

Look closer.

Tiny pits and lines are a natural pearl’s own history.

The cleaner the surface, the rarer the pearl.

The pearls of the Gulf

Prized, because the sea made them alone.

“Some of the best natural pearls in the world came from the oysters living in the warm, shallow waters of the Gulf.”

Our History, Emirates NBD Pearl Museum

The pearls of the Gulf were natural pearls, found by divers, not farmed. They “came to represent wealth and power from the shores of India to the Mediterranean and beyond.”

Natural, cultured or fake?

Three pearls can look alike. Only one is natural.

  • NaturalFormed by an oyster in the wild, with no human help.
  • CulturedGrown in a farmed oyster, after a person places a bead or a piece of tissue inside it.
  • ImitationMade by people, from glass, plastic or shell, and coated to look like pearl.
