The shells were opened the next morning.

Most shells held nothing. The few pearls they found were gathered and kept on board, in the captain’s care, until the merchants came.

The merchants

Then the merchants came.

“The pearl merchant’s launch was the antithesis of the diving dhow.”

The merchant’s tally book

Weighed, then written down.

Merchants kept careful books of what they bought, from whom, and for how much.

Many also advanced money to captains before the season, to be settled in pearls.

Weighing on board, reimagined. The tally book stays closed.

Grading

Sorted by size, through brass.

Pearls were sorted through a set of brass sieves, each pierced with holes of a different size. What stayed in each sieve belonged together. Then each grade was weighed, and shape, colour and lustre were judged by eye.

Brass pearl sieves, reimagined for this page. What makes a pearl special

Onward from the Gulf

From the launch, the pearls went out into the world.

“Pearls came to represent wealth and power from the shores of India to the Mediterranean and beyond.”

Our History, Emirates NBD Pearl Museum

And wealth came back. “The profitable trading within the Gulf including the export of pearls, brought in gold and silver coins struck by the Sultans and Mughal rulers of northern India, especially their gold Muhurs.”

Zenith and demise

Some of the best natural pearls in the world came from these waters.

Then came the cultured pearl, grown in a farmed oyster, and the old trade could not compete.

In 1949, the last pearling fleet of any size put into Dubai.
